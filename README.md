# ✈️ Aviation Safety Analysis Using Historical Airplane Crash Data

## 📌 Project Overview
The global aviation industry is one of the most complex and safety-critical transportation systems. Despite continuous technological advancements and strict regulatory oversight from organizations such as the **International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO)** and the **Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)**, aviation accidents still occur.

This project leverages the **"Airplane Crash Data Since 1908"** dataset to analyze historical aviation accidents and extract actionable insights. The goal is to build a **data-driven aviation safety analysis framework** that supports proactive decision-making and improves safety outcomes.

---

## 📊 Background
Airlines transport millions of passengers each year across diverse environments and operational conditions. Accidents can arise due to:

- Mechanical failures  
- Human error  
- Environmental conditions  
- Operational issues  

The dataset contains:
- Crash dates and locations  
- Aircraft types  
- Airline operators  
- Fatality counts  
- Accident summaries  

This makes it a valuable resource for **long-term trend analysis and risk assessment**.

---

## ⚠️ Core Business Problem
Aviation stakeholders currently lack an **integrated analytical system** to systematically analyze crash data across:

- Aircraft types  
- Geographic regions  
- Airline operators  
- Time periods  

This results in:
- Reactive rather than predictive safety improvements  
- Limited visibility into long-term safety trends  
- Difficulty identifying high-risk operations  

---

## 🌍 Problem Context
Airplane crashes have significant consequences:

- Loss of life and injuries  
- Financial losses for airlines and insurers  
- Operational disruptions  
- Increased regulatory pressure  
- Damage to public trust  

A robust analytical framework is needed to transform raw data into **meaningful, actionable insights**.

---

## 🔍 Analytical Problem
The key analytical challenge is to identify **patterns, causes, and risk factors** associated with aviation accidents.

### Key Questions:
- What are the trends in airplane crashes over time?
- Which aircraft types have higher accident frequencies?
- Are certain regions more prone to crashes?
- Which factors contribute most to fatalities?
- Are there high-risk airlines or aircraft models?

---

## 🎯 Business Objectives
This project aims to develop a **decision support system** for aviation safety.

### Objectives:
- Analyze historical trends in accidents and fatalities  
- Identify patterns by aircraft, operator, location, and time  
- Determine key contributing factors to crashes  
- Build predictive models for accident risk  
- Provide actionable safety recommendations
